Equilibrium solutions and stability

Stability
Consider the autonomous equation \[\frac{dx}{dt}=e^{-x}(x^3-5x^2+8x-4).\] Find all equilibrium solutions and determine their stability.
Your writing is saved in this browser.
No hints viewed
Step 1Find the equilibrium values
Since \(e^{-x}>0\) for every real \(x\), equilibria come from the polynomial. Factor it: \[x^3-5x^2+8x-4=(x-1)(x-2)^2.\] Which values of \(x\) make the right-hand side vanish?
Step 2Construct the sign chart
Write \(F(x)=e^{-x}(x-1)(x-2)^2\). The exponential is positive and the squared factor is nonnegative, so away from equilibria the sign is determined by \(x-1\): \[\begin{array}{c|ccc} \text{Interval}&(-\infty,1)&(1,2)&(2,\infty)\\\hline \operatorname{sgn}F&-&+&+\\ \text{Direction}&\leftarrow&\rightarrow&\rightarrow \end{array}\] Remember that \(x'(t)>0\) means solutions move toward larger \(x\).
Step 3Classify stability from both sides
At \(x=1\), the arrows point away on both sides. At \(x=2\), they point toward \(2\) from the left but away from \(2\) on the right. For two-sided stability, motion away on either side is enough to make an equilibrium unstable.

An equilibrium is stable when nearby solutions on both sides move toward it (or remain sufficiently close). If solutions move away from it on either side, it is unstable. Apply this at \(x=1,2,3,4\).

PROBLEM 03
Prove linear independence
Wronskian

  
Use the Wronskian to prove that the functions
\[f(x)=e^x,\qquad g(x)=xe^x,\qquad h(x)=e^{-x}\]
are linearly independent on \(\mathbb R\).

  
  
  
Your writing is saved in this browser.

  
No hints viewed

  
Step 1Differentiate each function twice
For three functions the Wronskian uses their values, first derivatives, and second derivatives:
\[\begin{array}{c|ccc}
& e^x & xe^x & e^{-x}\\\hline
\text{first derivative}&e^x&(x+1)e^x&-e^{-x}\\
\text{second derivative}&e^x&(x+2)e^x&e^{-x}
\end{array}\]
Double-check the product rule for \((xe^x)'\) and \((xe^x)''\).
Step 2Form and simplify the determinant
Set up
\[W(f,g,h)=\begin{vmatrix}
e^x&xe^x&e^{-x}\\
e^x&(x+1)e^x&-e^{-x}\\
e^x&(x+2)e^x&e^{-x}
\end{vmatrix}.\]
Factoring exponentials from columns gives a factor \(e^x\). Then subtract row 1 from rows 2 and 3 to get the smaller determinant
\[e^x\begin{vmatrix}1&x&1\\0&1&-2\\0&2&0\end{vmatrix}.\]
Step 3Explain why the result implies independence
The determinant simplifies to
\[W(f,g,h)=4e^x.\]
Since this is nonzero for every real \(x\), explain what the Wronskian criterion implies about \(f,g,h\).

Show the complete solution Check after attempting the problem
By the product rule,

\[f'=e^x,\quad g'=(x+1)e^x,\quad h'=-e^{-x},\]
\[f''=e^x,\quad g''=(x+2)e^x,\quad h''=e^{-x}.\]

Then

\[\begin{aligned}
W(f,g,h)
&=\begin{vmatrix}e^x&xe^x&e^{-x}\\e^x&(x+1)e^x&-e^{-x}\\e^x&(x+2)e^x&e^{-x}\end{vmatrix}\\
&=e^x\begin{vmatrix}1&x&1\\0&1&-2\\0&2&0\end{vmatrix}
=\boxed{4e^x}.
\end{aligned}\]

Since \(4e^x\ne0\) everywhere, the three functions are linearly independent on \(\mathbb R\).
